Science & near-death experiences

1975: science began to catch up with the book

In 1920, Toward the Light! described in detail what happens when a human being dies: how the spirit separates from the body, the dwellings that receive it, the review of the life just lived, and the loving helpers who meet it. Fifty-five years later, a young philosopher-turned-psychiatrist in the United States began publishing accounts that startled the world — because they said much the same thing, in the language of clinical research.

The man was Dr. Raymond Moody. After years of studying people who had been declared clinically dead and revived, he coined the term near-death experience (NDE) and, in 1975, published Life After Life — a book that ignited a lasting debate in both science and spirituality.

Moody's work was not vague speculation. He carefully documented more than 150 accounts. Across cultures, ages and beliefs, the same themes emerged:

  1. Leaving the body

    Many described floating above themselves, watching doctors and family below — calm, detached, as if the body were just a shell.

  2. The tunnel of light

    A passage, at the end of it a radiant, welcoming light — not just bright, but alive, overflowing with warmth, peace and love.

  3. Encounters with beings

    Departed loved ones or luminous figures of compassion — appearing, regardless of belief, as guides. Not to judge, but to welcome.

  4. The life review

    Not a reel of memories, but reliving one's life through the eyes of others — feeling the hurt, the joy, the ripple of every choice.

  5. Overwhelming love

    A love beyond anything imaginable. Complete acceptance. As if they had returned home.

  6. A boundary

    A line, a gate, a threshold. Crossing meant staying forever. Most were told: “Not yet.” And in an instant, they returned.

Not every account brought comfort — a small number described darkness, fear, a void. Why bliss for some and terror for others is a question that still lingers.

A prize from the Foundation

In the 1980s, Dr. Raymond Moody visited Denmark, where the Vandrer mod Lyset Foundation — the international foundation behind Toward the Light! — awarded him its prize in recognition of his pioneering research. At the occasion he was presented with the book itself, which addresses many of the very questions his research raises: Who are we? Why are we here? And where are we all going?

What about the skeptics?

Critics have proposed oxygen deprivation, brain seizures, or hallucination. Yet the research has only deepened. Pioneers such as Dr. Elisabeth Kübler-Ross, Dr. Bruce Greyson, and cardiologist Dr. Pim van Lommel — whose prospective study appeared in The Lancet in 2001 — have kept the central question open and serious: consciousness may not be confined to the brain.

The parallels speak for themselves. The conclusions are yours to draw.
