Jesus in Toward the Light!

For many readers, the most moving part of the book is its account of Jesus of Nazareth. More than twenty chapters of Ardor's Account are devoted to his life — retold, as the book states, by intelligences who witnessed it — followed by the Speech of Christ, in which he addresses every human being directly.

The chapters on Jesus' life

Each chapter answers one question, honestly and without dogma:

  • Was the birth of Jesus supernatural? Was he conceived by the Holy Ghost?
  • What were Jesus' own thoughts concerning his mission on Earth?
  • How are we to understand the baptism of Jesus?
  • How should we interpret the temptation in the wilderness?
  • Did Jesus teach of an eternal hell?
  • Did Jesus perform miracles — healing the sick, raising the dead?
  • Was Jesus able to drive out unclean spirits?
  • How should we interpret the Transfiguration on the Mount?
  • Why did Jesus refuse to baptize the people who sought him?
  • Was it predetermined that Judas should betray him?
  • What were the words spoken by Jesus before the Council?
  • What were the words spoken by Jesus before he died on the cross?
  • Was the resurrection bodily or spiritual?
  • How should we interpret the “miracle of Pentecost”?
  • How was Saul converted from persecutor to preacher?
  • In what manner did the doctrine of atonement arise?

Christ's own words

“Christ, the eldest of the Youngest, speak unto you. Hear me, for I speak in the name of our God and Father! … Follow me, all who suffer, all who labour with toil and trouble, all who sigh and groan under heavy burdens! Follow me all! For I shall lead you unto our Father, I shall give you rest in His embrace!” — Speech of Christ

Why it matters

The book does not ask Christians to abandon Christ — it asks them to meet him again, “freed from the inventions and distortions of the centuries.” Readers from many backgrounds — Christian, searching, or simply curious — find here a coherent and deeply human portrait of Jesus, and a promise that extends to every human being without exception.
